Classical Harmonic Oscillator

A classical harmonic oscillator is a simple system where a particle (like a mass on a spring) moves back and forth because of a restoring force. The force follows Hooke’s law:

F = -kx

Where:

  • F = Force pulling it back to the center
  • k = Stiffness of the spring
  • x = Displacement from the center

How it behaves:

  • The particle can have any energy — no restrictions.
  • It moves in a smooth, continuous path (like a sine wave).
  • The total energy includes both kinetic and potential energy:

    E = ½mv² + ½kx²

  • At rest, the energy can be zero — the particle just sits still.

Example:

Think of a swing — you pull it back, let go, and it swings back and forth smoothly, losing energy slowly due to friction.

Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

Now, things get weird at the quantum level. When the system is small enough (like an atom), classical rules stop working, and quantum mechanics takes over.

Key differences:

  • Energy levels are discrete — the particle can’t have just any energy; it can only have specific "steps" of energy:

    En = ℏω(n + ½)

    Where:

    • n = 0, 1, 2, 3, … (quantum number)
    • ℏ = Reduced Planck’s constant
    • ω = Angular frequency
  • Zero-point energy — Even at the lowest energy state (n = 0), the particle still has some energy:

    E0 = ½ℏω

    So the particle is never truly at rest.

  • Uncertainty principle — You can't know both the exact position and momentum of the particle at the same time:

    ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2

    This makes the particle’s motion kind of “fuzzy.”

  • Wave nature — The particle behaves like both a particle and a wave, described by a wave function ψ(x) from Schrödinger’s equation:

    Ĥψ(x) = Eψ(x)

Why Classical Fails and Quantum Takes Over

So why do we need quantum mechanics at all?

👉 In classical physics:

  • Energy can be any value — but in quantum physics, energy is quantized (only certain values are allowed).
  • A particle at rest should have zero energy — but in quantum physics, even the lowest state has energy (zero-point energy).
  • Classical physics says you can measure position and momentum at the same time — quantum mechanics says nope, uncertainty principle.
  • Classical physics works fine for big objects — but at small scales (like atoms), it completely breaks down.

Key Differences (Simple Version)

Feature Classical Quantum
Energy levels Continuous Discrete
Lowest energy Zero Non-zero (zero-point energy)
Motion Smooth and predictable Fuzzy and uncertain
Behavior Acts like a particle Acts like both particle and wave
